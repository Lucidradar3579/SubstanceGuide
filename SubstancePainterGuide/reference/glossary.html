Substance 3D Painter & Roblox Glossary

Difficulty: Beginner Time: 20 minutes Software: Substance 3D Painter Roblox Relevance: High

This glossary contains definitions of key terms used throughout this guide and in the Substance 3D Painter and Roblox communities. Understanding these terms will help you follow tutorials, communicate with other artists, and navigate documentation more effectively.

How to Use This Glossary

Terms are organized alphabetically for easy reference. Each term includes a clear, concise definition and, where relevant, examples or related terms. Refer back to this glossary whenever you encounter unfamiliar terminology.

A - D

Ambient Occlusion (AO)

A shading method that calculates how exposed each point in a scene is to ambient lighting. Creates soft shadows in crevices, corners, and where objects meet.

Base Color

The fundamental color of a material before lighting, shadows, or other effects are applied. In PBR workflows, this represents the diffuse color of the material.

Baking

The process of transferring high-detail information from a high-polygon model to a low-polygon model through texture maps (normal, AO, curvature, etc.).

Bitmap

A digital image composed of pixels arranged in a grid. Common texture formats include PNG, JPG, and TGA.

Blend Mode

A setting that determines how two layers interact with each other. Common modes include Normal, Multiply, Overlay, Screen, and Add.

Channel

In texture maps, a channel refers to a specific component of the image (Red, Green, Blue, Alpha). In PBR, different channels store different material properties.

Curvature Map

A texture map that highlights convex (peaks) and concave (valleys) areas of a model. Useful for adding wear, dirt, or highlighting edges.

Diffuse

The base color of a material that represents how it appears under pure white light without any directional lighting effects.

Displacement Map

A texture map that physically displaces vertices of a mesh to create actual geometry, rather than just simulating depth like a normal map.

Emissive

The amount of light that a material emits itself, rather than reflecting from other light sources. Used for glowing materials.

Fill Layer

A layer in Substance Painter that uniformly fills the texture with a solid color, gradient, or pattern.

Normal Map

A texture map that simulates surface detail by altering how light interacts with the surface, creating the illusion of bumps and dents without adding geometry.

PBR (Physically Based Rendering)

A rendering approach that aims to simulate how light interacts with materials based on their physical properties, resulting in more realistic and consistent materials.

Procedural Texture

A texture generated mathematically rather than from image files, allowing for infinite detail and resolution independence.

Roughness

A measure of how rough or smooth a material's surface is. A value of 0 is perfectly smooth (mirror-like), while 1 is completely rough (matte).

Smart Material

A pre-configured collection of layers and effects in Substance Painter that can be applied to create complex materials quickly.

Specular

The mirror-like reflection of light from a surface. In PBR workflows, specular is often derived from the metalness value.

Stencil

A mask used to limit where paint or effects can be applied, allowing for precise control over where textures appear.

Texel Density

The number of texture pixels per unit of 3D space. Consistent texel density ensures uniform texture resolution across a model.

Texture Set

A collection of texture maps (Base Color, Normal, Roughness, etc.) that together define the complete appearance of a material.

UV Mapping

The process of flattening a 3D model's surface onto a 2D plane so that textures can be applied correctly. UVs are the 2D coordinates used for this mapping.

Value

The lightness or darkness of a color, independent of its hue or saturation. In grayscale images, value represents the brightness.

E - K

Height Map

A texture map that stores elevation information, where white represents the highest points and black represents the lowest points.

ID Map

A texture map that uses different colors to identify different materials or sections of a model, useful for creating masks.

Instance

In Roblox, an instance is any object that exists in the game world, such as a Part, MeshPart, Script, or GUI element.

Layer

In Substance Painter, a layer is a container for texture information that can be stacked, blended, and masked to create complex materials.

Material

The substance or substances that an object is made of, which determines how it interacts with light and other environmental factors.

Metalness

A value that indicates how metallic a material is. A value of 0 is a non-metal (dielectric), while 1 is a pure metal.

Mipmap

A series of progressively lower resolution versions of a texture used for efficient rendering at different distances.

Normal (Tangent Space)

The standard format for normal maps where the RGB values represent perturbations relative to the surface orientation.

Opacity

The degree to which a material allows light to pass through it. 0 is completely transparent, 1 is completely opaque.

PBR Workflow

The process of creating materials using Physically Based Rendering principles, typically involving Base Color, Normal, Roughness, and Metalness maps.

Particle System

In Roblox, a system for creating and managing large numbers of small, similar objects (like sparks, smoke, or debris) efficiently.

Photogrammetry

The process of creating 3D models from photographs, often used to create highly realistic textures and geometry.

Pixel

The smallest unit of a digital image, representing a single point of color.

Point Light

A light source that emits light equally in all directions from a single point in space.

Polygon

A flat, multi-sided shape that forms the building blocks of 3D models. Triangles and quads are the most common polygon types.

Procedural Generation

The creation of content algorithmically rather than manually, often used for textures, terrain, and other repetitive elements.

Projection

The method used to apply a 2D texture onto a 3D surface. Common types include planar, cylindrical, spherical, and UV projection.

Rigging

The process of creating a skeleton and controls for a 3D model to enable animation.

Roblox Studio

The official development environment for creating Roblox games and experiences.

Shader

A program that runs on the GPU to calculate how pixels should be rendered based on lighting, material properties, and other factors.

Skybox

A large cube or sphere that surrounds a scene and displays a background image, often used to create distant environments.

Specular Workflow

An alternative to the metalness workflow where specular color and intensity are defined directly rather than derived from metalness.

Subdivision Surface

A technique for creating smooth surfaces by recursively subdividing and averaging the vertices of a polygonal mesh.

Texture Atlas

A single large texture that contains multiple smaller textures, used to reduce draw calls and improve performance.

Texture Filtering

The method used to determine the color of a texture when it's displayed at a size different from its original resolution.

Texture Resolution

The dimensions of a texture in pixels (width × height). Higher resolutions provide more detail but require more memory.

UV Island

A connected piece of a UV layout that represents a continuous section of the 3D model's surface.

UV Seam

The edge where two UV islands meet in the UV layout. Seams can cause visible artifacts if not handled properly.

Vector

A mathematical quantity with both magnitude and direction, often used to represent positions, velocities, and forces in 3D space.

L - R

Layer Stack

The ordered collection of layers in Substance Painter that determines how they blend together to create the final material appearance.

Level of Detail (LOD)

Technique of using different versions of a model with varying complexity based on distance from the camera to improve performance.

Lightmap

A pre-calculated texture that stores lighting information for static objects, allowing for complex lighting without real-time calculation.

Mask

A grayscale image used to control the visibility or intensity of layers or effects, where white shows the effect fully and black hides it completely.

Material ID

An identifier used to distinguish different materials within a single object, often used with ID maps for masking.

Metallic Workflow

A PBR workflow that uses a metalness map to define which parts of a material are metallic and which are non-metallic.

Normal Map Compression

Techniques for reducing the file size of normal maps while minimizing quality loss, important for game performance.

Object Space

A coordinate system where the origin is at the center of an individual object, rather than the world or camera.

Overdraw

The situation where the same pixel is drawn multiple times in a single frame, wasting GPU resources.

Parallax Occlusion Mapping

An advanced technique that simulates depth and self-occlusion in textures, providing more realistic results than standard normal maps.

Physically Based Rendering (PBR)

A rendering approach that aims to simulate how light interacts with materials based on their physical properties, resulting in more realistic and consistent materials.

Pixel Shader

A shader that calculates the color and other attributes of individual pixels.

Point Cloud

A set of data points in 3D space, often created by 3D scanners, used to represent the external surface of an object.

Post-Process

Effects applied after the main rendering pass, such as bloom, motion blur, or color grading.

Projection Painting

A technique where 2D images are projected onto a 3D model to guide texturing or painting.

PBR (Physically Based Rendering)

A rendering approach that aims to simulate how light interacts with materials based on their physical properties, resulting in more realistic and consistent materials.

Ray Tracing

A rendering technique that simulates the physical behavior of light to produce highly realistic lighting and reflections.

Reflection

The bouncing of light off a surface, which can be specular (mirror-like) or diffuse (scattered in many directions).

Refraction

The bending of light as it passes through a transparent material, such as glass or water.

Resolution

The level of detail in an image or model, typically measured in pixels for textures or polygons for 3D models.

Rim Light

Light that hits the edges of an object from behind, creating a bright outline that helps separate the object from the background.

Roughness Map

A texture map that stores roughness information, where black represents smooth areas and white represents rough areas.

Sample

In digital audio or imaging, a single measurement taken from a continuous signal at a specific point in time or space.

Saturation

The intensity or purity of a color. A fully saturated color is pure hue, while a desaturated color appears more gray.

Scalar

A single numerical value, as opposed to a vector or matrix which has multiple components.

Screen Space

A coordinate system where the origin is at the corner of the screen, used for post-processing effects and UI elements.

Seamless Texture

A texture that can be tiled repeatedly without visible seams or discontinuities at the edges.

Sharpening

A filter or process that increases the contrast between adjacent pixels to make edges appear more distinct.

Sky Light

Light that comes from the sky or environment, providing indirect illumination to objects in a scene.

Specular Map

A texture map that stores specular reflection information, controlling how shiny or reflective different areas of a material are.

Sphere

A three-dimensional geometric shape where all points on the surface are equidistant from the center.

SSAO (Screen Space Ambient Occlusion)

A real-time technique for approximating ambient occlusion in screen space, often used in games for performance.

Substance

The core technology behind Substance 3D Painter that allows for procedural, non-destructive texturing through parametric controls.
