Understanding Masks in Substance 3D Painter

Beginner → Advanced Time: 25 minutes Software: Substance 3D Painter Roblox Relevance: Essential

Masks are what give you precise control over where layers appear in your texture. Mastering masks is essential for creating realistic, professional-looking materials for your Roblox assets. This guide covers everything from basic black and white masks to advanced procedural masking techniques.

Why Masks Matter for Roblox Workflows

Masks allow you to place details exactly where they belong - wear on edges, dirt in crevices, scratches on high-contact areas - creating believable materials that enhance your Roblox assets without looking procedural or fake.

Mask Fundamentals

Understanding the basics of how masks work:

How Masks Work:

  • Masks are grayscale values (0-255 or 0.0-1.0)
  • Black (0) = Layer is invisible
  • White (255) = Layer is fully visible
  • Gray values = Partial transparency
  • Masks can be painted, procedurally generated, or imported
  • Roblox Tip: Think of masks as "stencils" that control where your material appears

Mask Types in Substance 3D Painter:

Black Mask

Starts completely hidden

Use when: You want to add details selectively

Workflow: Add Black Mask, then paint white where you want the layer to appear

White Mask

Starts completely visible

Use when: You want to remove details from specific areas

Workflow: Add White Mask, then paint black where you want to hide the layer

Additive Mask

Combines with existing mask

Use when: You want to add to an existing masked area

Example: Adding extra wear to already masked scratch areas

Subtractive Mask

Subtracts from existing mask

Use when: You want to remove from an existing masked area

Example: Creating clean areas within a dirty mask

Procedural Mask

Generated from mesh properties

Use when: You want automatic, intelligent placement

Examples: Curvature (edges), Position (height), Ambient Occlusion (crevices)

Creating and Editing Masks

How to create and modify masks for precise control:

Working with Masks in the Layer Stack

1

Adding a Mask to a Layer

Every layer can have a mask:

  • Select your layer in the layer stack
  • Click the "Add Mask" button (circle inside rectangle)
  • Choose Black Mask, White Mask, or Add Painted Mask
  • The mask appears as a white rectangle below your layer
  • Tip: You can also right-click layer > Add Black/White Mask

2

Editing Masks in 2D View

Paint directly on the UV layout:

  • Click on the mask thumbnail in layer stack
  • It will highlight to show you're editing the mask
  • Switch to 2D view (press "2" or click UV icon)
  • Paint with white to reveal, black to hide
  • Use erasers, shapes, and other tools
  • Tip: Press "M" to toggle between editing layer content and mask

3

Editing Masks in 3D View

Paint directly on your 3D model:

  • Click on the mask thumbnail in layer stack
  • Make sure you're in 3D view (press "3" or click mesh icon)
  • Paint with your brush - white reveals, black hides
  • All your 3D painting tools work on masks
  • Tip: Use projection modes for accurate placement

4

Mask Properties and Controls

Adjust mask behavior:

  • Mask opacity controls overall strength
  • You can invert masks (click the invert button)
  • Mask properties include blur, contrast, levels, etc.
  • These affect how the mask transitions between black and white

Procedural Masks: The Power of Intelligence

Let Substance Painter generate masks based on your mesh:

Common Procedural Masks for Roblox

The most useful procedurally generated masks:

Curvature Mask

Detects edges and corners on your mesh

  • White on convex/concave edges
  • Black on flat surfaces
  • Roblox Use: Edge wear, scratches, paint chipping on corners

Position Mask

Based on height/position of vertices

  • Can be set to highlight high points or low points
  • Great for dirt accumulation (low points) or wear (high points)
  • Roblox Use: Dirt in crevices, wear on raised surfaces, snow accumulation

Ambient Occlusion Mask

Based on how much light reaches areas

  • White in crevices and enclosed areas
  • Black on exposed, open areas
  • Roblox Use: Dirt, grime, moss in corners and joints

Thickness Mask

Based on mesh thickness

  • Thicker areas vs thinner areas
  • Useful for certain wear patterns
  • Roblox Use: Edge highlighting on thin materials

Normal Mask

Based on surface normal direction

  • Can isolate faces pointing in certain directions
  • Useful for directional effects
  • Roblox Use: Water accumulation on flat surfaces

Texture Map Mask

Based on existing texture maps

  • Use curvature map, position map, etc. as masks
  • Great for chaining effects
  • Roblox Use: Using baked curvature map to drive wear in Substance

Generating Procedural Masks

1

Adding a Procedural Mask Generator

Let Substance do the work:

  • Add Black Mask to your layer
  • Click the mask thumbnail to select it
  • In Properties panel, click "Generate Mask"
  • Choose from Curvature, Position, AO, etc.
  • Adjust generator parameters for desired effect
  • Tip: You can stack multiple procedural masks

2

Combining Procedural Masks

Create complex mask logic:

  • Add multiple generators to the same mask
  • Use Add/Subtract to combine them
  • Example: Curvature AND Position = edges that are also low points
  • Useful for very specific placement (e.g., rust on low edges)
3

Refining Procedural Masks

Adjust the generated mask:

  • After generating, you can still paint on the mask
  • Add levels adjustment to control range
  • Add blur to soften edges
  • Invert if needed
  • Tip: Generate at higher quality, then refine for best results

Practical Mask Examples for Roblox Assets

Real-world examples of how to use masks effectively:

Character and Clothing Examples

Masking techniques for wearable items:

Adding Wear to Roblox Clothing

1

Edge Wear on Sleeves and Hems

Natural wear on fabric edges:

  • Add Fill Layer for wear (lighter/darker color)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Generate mask using Curvature (for edges)
  • Optionally combine with Position (for specific height)
  • Adjust opacity to 10-30% for subtle wear
  • Roblox Tip: Focus on areas that would bend or rub

2

Dirt and Sweat Accumulation

Where grime naturally collects:

  • Add Fill Layer for dirt (dark/brownish)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Generate mask using Position (low points)
  • Optionally combine with Ambient Occlusion (crevices)
  • Adjust opacity and blending (try Multiply at 15-25%)
  • Roblox Tip: Think about where sweat would collect during activity

3

Elbow and Knee Wear

Specific joint wear patterns:

  • Add Fill Layer for wear
  • Add Black Mask
  • Use custom mask (paint or procedural)
  • Focus on outer edges of joints
  • Consider both curvature (edges) and position (prominent bones)
  • Roblox Tip: Reference real clothing or observe how joints bend

Prop and Environment Examples

Masking techniques for objects and scenery:

Creating Realistic Metal Props

1

Scratches and Edge Wear

Where metal shows use:

  • Add Fill Layer for scratches (lighter metal)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Generate mask using Curvature (edges and corners)
  • Add Noise generator to mask for variation
  • Set blending to Normal or Overlay at 15-25% opacity
  • Roblox Tip: Vary scratch direction and length for realism

2

Dirt and Grime Accumulation

Where dirt naturally collects:

  • Add Fill Layer for dirt (dark/brown)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Generate mask using Position (low points)
  • Combine with Ambient Occlusion (crevices and joints)
  • Adjust opacity and blending (Multiply often works well)
  • Roblox Tip: Consider weather exposure and handling patterns

3

Rust and Corrosion

Where metal degrades:

  • Add Fill Layer for rust color (orange/brown)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Generate using Position (low points where moisture collects)
  • Combine with Curvature (edges where coating wears)
  • Adjust roughness where rust occurs
  • Roblox Tip: Rust follows water patterns - think about drainage

Roblox-Specific Examples

Masking techniques for platform-specific needs:

Stud Wear and Handling Marks

1

Connection Point Wear

Where studs and connectors show use:

  • Add Fill Layer for wear (smoother/shinier)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Use procedural mask or paint mask
  • Focus on areas that experience friction
  • Consider both curvature (edges) and custom painting
  • Roblox Tip: Reference how players actually connect and disconnect items

2

UI Element Highlights

For interactive elements:

  • Add Fill Layer for highlight (lighter color)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Create custom shape mask (button outlines, etc.)
  • Or use texture mask from UI design
  • Adjust opacity for subtle hover/active states
  • Roblox Tip: Test how highlights look in different lighting conditions

3

Environmental Variation

Avoiding the "too perfect" look:

  • Add Fill Layer for variation (slight color/noise change)
  • Add Black Mask
  • Use Noise generator with low contrast
  • Or use Texture Map mask from variation texture
  • Set blending to subtle mode (Overlay, Soft Light) at low opacity
  • Roblox Tip: Subtle variation prevents tiling look in large areas

Advanced Masking Techniques

Pro-level mask control for complex materials:

Mask Stacking and Layering

Combining multiple masks for sophisticated control:

  • Add multiple masks to the same layer (stack them)
  • Each mask can be edited independently
  • Use blending modes between masks (Multiply, Add, etc.)
  • Example: Base mask (curvature) + painted details (specific scratches)

Animated and Dynamic Masks

For materials that change over time:

  • While Substance Painter is static, you can plan for animation
  • Create masks that can be animated externally (in shader or material)
  • Example: Mask for where snow accumulates that can be animated to melt
  • Roblox Note: For true animation, consider using multiple texture sets or shaders in Roblox

Mask Presets and Libraries

Saving time with reusable masks:

  • Save frequently used mask configurations
  • Example: "Edge Wear Mask" (curvature + noise)
  • Example: "Dirt in Crevices Mask" (position + AO)
  • Save as smart materials or as layer configurations
  • Roblox Tip: Create a library of go-to masks for common wear patterns
